How results are calculated

What InverseHub compares on each compliance path, in plain words. The calculation on your project page shows the same steps with your numbers.

  • Performance cost index (ASHRAE 90.1-2016 and later)

Energy cost first

Every path starts from the annual energy cost of each design, from the utility rates in the reports. On-site renewable energy and exceptional calculations you entered in SimReader are subtracted from the proposed design. New off-site renewable energy is subtracted only from the proposed design’s GHG emissions, for LEED v4 with the 2024 energy update.

Performance cost index (ASHRAE 90.1-2016 and later)

The proposed design complies when its performance cost index is at or below the target:

  • PCI = proposed building performance ÷ baseline building performance, where both are annual energy costs.
  • PCIt = (baseline unregulated energy cost + BPF × baseline regulated energy cost − PRE) ÷ baseline building performance.

Where:

  • BPF is the code’s building performance factor for the building type and climate zone. With several building area types, InverseHub uses the area-weighted average.
  • PRE is the energy cost of the on-site renewable energy the code requires (90.1-2022 and the codes based on it). It’s zero otherwise.
  • On-site renewables count toward the PCI up to 5% of the baseline building performance; with a PRE, only renewables beyond the required system count.

Energy index. With the energy method, the same formulas use site energy instead of cost. Codes with their own energy rules use their own BPF tables and conversion factors: source energy for NYC 2020, site energy for NYC 2025 and New York State 2025. ASHRAE 90.1-2022 and Oregon 2025 offer site energy, source energy or carbon. If you enter local conversion factors for electricity and gas, InverseHub recalculates the BPF from them with the code’s coefficients.

Earlier ASHRAE 90.1 editions (2007 to 2013)

The Performance Rating Method compares the proposed design’s energy cost with the baseline building’s. It passes when the proposed cost is lower. With the energy method, it compares site energy.

Energy Cost Budget Method

The design energy cost (DEC) of the proposed design must be at or below the energy cost budget (ECB), the budget building’s annual energy cost.

  • From 90.1-2016 on, on-site renewables are credited up to 5% of the budget.
  • In 90.1-2022, the budget is reduced by the required energy credits: ECB × (1 − credits ÷ 1000 × the additions and alterations factor). The required on-site solar’s cost (PRE) comes off the budget, and only renewables beyond it are credited.

Total Building Performance (IECC)

The proposed design’s annual energy cost must be at most a share of the standard reference design’s:

  • 100% for IECC 2006 and 2009; 85% for IECC 2012 to 2021; 80% for Chicago 2022; 85% for New York State 2020.
  • For IECC 2018 and 2021, and Chicago 2022, on-site renewables count up to 5% of the standard reference design’s energy cost. For the other editions, they’re subtracted from the proposed cost.

IgCC

  • IgCC 2012 and 2015: the zero energy performance index (zEPI) compares source energy use intensities: zEPI = 57 (2012) or 52 (2015) × proposed EUI ÷ standard reference EUI. It must be at most 51 (2012) or 50 (2015). The proposed design’s CO₂e emissions are checked too. On-site renewable energy is subtracted from the proposed design’s energy and emissions.
  • IgCC 2021: the cost PCI and the source-energy zEPI against their targets, which the building’s renewable fraction lowers. Also checked: CO₂e emissions, and the PCI without renewables against the ASHRAE 90.1-2019 target.
  • Source energy and CO₂e factors come from the code. Electricity uses the factors of your project’s EPA eGRID subregion.

Unmet load hours

SimReader and InverseHub show the heating and cooling unmet load hours of each design, from the first file of each list. They flag a total over 300 hours in red.

Units

SimReader converts every report to the same units before any calculation. See SimReader units.
