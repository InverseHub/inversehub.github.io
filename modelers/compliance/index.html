Show compliance

Add the calculations your compliance path needs, then let InverseHub write the conclusion. Both follow the energy code and rating system in Energy Model Information.

The two steps, on every path

  1. Calculations: select + on the calculations card. Its name follows the path: Performance Calculations, Energy Cost Budget Calculations, Total Building Performance Calculations or zEPI Calculations.
  2. Conclusion: select + on Conclusion. InverseHub checks the results against the code and writes a short pass or fail statement: the result with its numbers, then the requirement. For example: “Pass. The Performance Cost Index (PCI) is 0.42, 20.7% below the target (PCIt) of 0.53. It must be at most the PCIt.” When the design passes, it adds that the design meets the path’s performance requirement. It also notes that the code’s mandatory provisions apply too, since InverseHub doesn’t check them.

To redo either, delete it with its trash icon and add it again; the saved text doesn’t update by itself. Change the inputs with the edit icon on the calculation.

The review page’s summary shows the conclusion’s key figures, such as LEED points, the PCI against the PCIt or the 179D deduction. They’re saved when you add the conclusion, and editing its text keeps them. For a conclusion added before October 2026, add it again to show them.

Performance Rating Method (ASHRAE 90.1 Appendix G)

90.1-2016 and later, and the codes built on them

Compliance is a performance cost index (PCI) at or below its target (PCIt). The target comes from the code’s building performance factors (BPFs).

  1. On Performance Calculations, select +.
  2. Choose the Performance Index Calculation Method:
    • Performance Cost Index Method: energy cost, as the standard requires.
    • Performance Energy Index Method: site energy. Codes with their own energy rules name their method here instead. For example, NYC 2020 uses source energy, NYC 2025 site energy, and ASHRAE 90.1-2022 and Oregon 2025 let you pick site energy, source energy or carbon, optionally with local conversion factors.
  3. Choose the BPF Calculation: the code’s defaults for your climate zone, or a Custom BPF.
  4. Enter the floor area of each Building Area Type. The effective BPF is the area-weighted average. BPFs cover conditioned floor area only.
  5. For 90.1-2022 and the codes based on it, enter the annual output of any on-site solar the code requires. Its value comes off the target, and only renewables beyond it count toward the 5% renewables credit.
  6. Select Add Calculations.

The card then shows the result with each step of the calculation.

If you pick a method the code doesn’t allow, a red note says so in the dialog, the calculation and the conclusion. The conclusion then leaves out the compliance sentence: the result is for reference only.

90.1-2007, 2010 and 2013

There are no BPFs. The conclusion compares the proposed design’s energy cost with the baseline’s: it passes when the proposed cost is lower. With the energy method, it compares site energy instead.

Energy Cost Budget Method (ASHRAE 90.1)

Compliance is the design energy cost (the proposed design’s) at or below the energy cost budget (the budget building’s).

  1. On Energy Cost Budget Calculations, select +. For 90.1-2022 and the codes based on it, enter the energy credits required, the additions and alterations factor and the annual output of the required on-site solar. Earlier editions need no inputs here.
  2. Add the conclusion.

On-site renewables are subtracted from the design energy cost. From 90.1-2016 on, they’re credited up to 5% of the budget.

Requirements the edition sets beyond the cost comparison are listed in the calculation as “also required, not checked here”.

Total Building Performance (IECC)

Compliance is the proposed design’s annual energy cost at or below a share of the standard reference design’s:

CodeLimitOn-site renewables
IECC 2006, 2009100%Subtracted from the proposed cost
IECC 2012, 201585%Subtracted from the proposed cost
IECC 2018, 202185%Credited up to 5% of the reference design’s cost
Chicago 202280%Credited up to 5%
New York State 202085%Subtracted from the proposed cost

Add Total Building Performance Calculations (+), then the conclusion.

IgCC Performance Option

  1. On zEPI Calculations, select +. Choose your project’s EPA eGRID subregion: it sets the electricity’s source energy and carbon factors. For IgCC 2021, also enter the building areas.
  2. Add the conclusion. InverseHub checks the zero energy performance index (zEPI) against its target and the carbon (CO₂e) emissions.

InverseHub can’t conclude for IgCC 2018 yet, because its tables from ASHRAE 189.1-2017 are still to be entered.

LEED and other programs

For LEED rating systems, the conclusion gives the points for the Optimize Energy Performance credit under the LEED version you chose. The Massachusetts pathways and 179D have their own conclusions too.

For LEED ID+C on the 2024 Update, adding the conclusion first asks which baseline envelope you modeled: New construction or Existing conditions before renovation. The conclusion and the review page’s summary then give the points for that envelope only. To switch, delete the conclusion and add it again.

On the 2024 Update, new off-site renewable energy entered in SimReader counts toward the GHG emissions reduction only, not toward energy cost or source energy. Each system’s emissions offset uses its grid region’s eGRID factor. The conclusion says how much of the reduction it gives. The project page shows each system’s offset and, for your Renewable Energy credit, its share of the proposed design’s site energy and GHG emissions before renewables. InverseHub doesn’t calculate the Renewable Energy credit’s points.

Unmet load hours

When the upload includes unmet load hours, an Unmet Load Hours card shows them for both designs. It’s hidden until you switch it on, both on the review page and in the PDF.
